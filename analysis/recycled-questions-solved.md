## Question 1 · Clocks

**Appeared in:** NIMCET 2018 (Q80, printed again as Q85) → NIMCET 2022 (Q87) · gap 4 years

&nbsp;

At what time, in minutes, between 3 o'clock and 4 o'clock, will both the hands of a clock coincide?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) 5 1⁄11 min past 3

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) 12 4⁄11 min past 3

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) 13 4⁄11 min past 3

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(4) 16 4⁄11 min past 3

&nbsp;

### Solution

**Step 1.** At 3:00 the hour hand is at 90° and the minute hand is at 0°. They will coincide when the minute hand has made up this 90°.

**Step 2.** The minute hand moves 6° per minute, the hour hand 0.5° per minute, so the minute hand gains 6 − 0.5 = **5.5° per minute**.

**Step 3.** Time needed = 90 ÷ 5.5 = 180⁄11 = **16 4⁄11 minutes**.

> **Answer: (4) — 16 4⁄11 minutes past 3** (same option in both years)

**Remember:** the hands coincide at **60H⁄11 minutes past H**. They are opposite at 60(H ± 6)⁄11.

## Question 2 · Logical puzzle set — "Five houses in a row"

**Appeared in:** NIMCET 2009 (Q61–Q65) → NIMCET 2012 (Q86–Q88) · gap 3 years

&nbsp;

Five houses lettered A, B, C, D and E are built in a row next to each other, in the order A, B, C, D, E. Each house has a coloured roof and a coloured chimney, painted under these rules:

1. The roof must be green, red or yellow.
2. The chimney must be white, black or red.
3. No house may have a chimney the same colour as its roof.
4. No house may use any colour that the house next to it uses.
5. House E has a green roof.
6. House B has a red roof and a black chimney.

&nbsp;

### Work out the arrangement first (do this once, then answer every question)

**Step 1.** B uses red and black. Its neighbours A and C may use neither. A chimney can only be white, black or red, so **A and C both have white chimneys**, and their roofs must be green or yellow.

**Step 2.** D sits between C and E. E's roof is green, so D's roof is red or yellow. D cannot copy C's white chimney, so **D's chimney is black or red** — and not the same as D's own roof. D's roof also cannot equal C's roof.

**Step 3.** E's chimney cannot be green (its own roof) and cannot be any colour D uses.

| House | Roof | Chimney |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| A | green or yellow | **white** |
| B | red | black |
| C | green or yellow | **white** |
| D | red or yellow (≠ C's roof) | black or red (≠ own roof) |
| E | green | whatever D does not use |

&nbsp;

**(a) 2009 Q61 / 2012 Q88 — What is the maximum number of green roofs?**

E is green. A and C can both be green: they are not next to each other, not next to E, and D is never green. B is red.

> **Answer: 3 → option (3)**

&nbsp;

**(b) 2009 Q62 / 2012 Q87 — If house C has a yellow roof, which of the following must be true?**

D's roof cannot be yellow → D's roof is red → D's chimney cannot be red → D's chimney is black. E may now use neither red nor black.

> **Answer: House E has a white chimney → option (1)**

&nbsp;

**(c) 2009 Q63 / 2012 Q86 — Which of the following is true?**

A and C always have white chimneys.

> **Answer: At least two houses have white chimneys → option (3)**

(Only B is guaranteed a black chimney; only B a red roof; only E a green roof.)

&nbsp;

**(d) 2009 Q64 — Which statement is false?**

"House A and house C have different colour chimneys" — both are white.

> **Answer: option (2)**

&nbsp;

**(e) 2009 Q65 — Which roof–chimney combinations are possible: (i) red roof & black chimney, (ii) yellow roof & red chimney, (iii) yellow roof & black chimney?**

(i) is house B. (ii) and (iii) are both possible for D when C's roof is green.

> **Answer: I, II and III → option (1)**

## Question 3 · Blood relations set — "Family of six, two married couples"

**Appeared in:** NIMCET 2015 (Q12–Q15) → NIMCET 2017 (Q92–Q94) → NIMCET 2021 (Q84, Q86, Q87, Q105) · gaps 2 and 4 years

&nbsp;

In a family of six persons A, B, C, D, E and F there are two married couples.

- D is the grandmother of A and the mother of B.
- C is the wife of B and the mother of F.
- F is the granddaughter of E.

&nbsp;

### Build the family tree

**Step 1.** D is a grandmother and E is F's grandfather → **D and E are couple 1** (the grandparents).

**Step 2.** B is D's child and C is B's wife → **B and C are couple 2**. F is C's child, so F is the daughter of B and C.

**Step 3.** A is D's grandchild. The only child of D in the family is B, so **A is also a child of B and C** — A and F are siblings.

**Step 4.** Genders known: D, C, F female; B, E male. **A's gender is never stated.**

&nbsp;

**(a) What is C to A?**

> **Answer: Mother → 2015 option (3), 2017 option (2)**

&nbsp;

**(b) How many male members are there?**

B and E are male. A may be male or female.

> **Answer: Cannot be determined → 2015 option (4)** (a key that says "two" has assumed A is female — the question does not say so)

&nbsp;

**(c) Who is one of the couples?**

> **Answer: DE → option (2) in both years**

&nbsp;

**(d) Which of the following is true? — A is brother of F / A is sister of F / B has two daughters**

All three depend on A's gender, which is unknown.

> **Answer: None of these → option (4) in both years**

## Question 4 · Logical puzzle set — "Nine individuals, three committees"

**Appeared in:** NIMCET 2018 (Q113–Q115) → NIMCET 2020 (Q21–Q22) · gap 2 years

&nbsp;

Nine individuals Z, Y, X, W, V, U, T, S and R are the only candidates who can serve on three committees A, B and C (called K1, K2, K3 in 2020). Each serves on exactly one committee. Committee A must have exactly one member more than committee B. Committee C may be empty.

- Z, Y and X cannot serve on committee A.
- W, V and U cannot serve on committee B.
- T, S and R cannot serve on committee C.

&nbsp;

### The three facts to keep in front of you

- |A| = |B| + 1, and |A| + |B| + |C| = 9.
- T, S, R must be on A or B (never C).
- Z, Y, X must be on B or C. W, V, U must be on A or C.

&nbsp;

**(a) 2018 Q113 / 2020 Q21 — If T and Z are the only members of B, how many serve on C?**

|B| = 2 → |A| = 3 → |C| = 4. S and R cannot go to C and are not on B, so they are on A with one of W, V, U. C gets Y, X and the other two of W, V, U.

> **Answer: 4 → option (2)**

&nbsp;

**(b) 2018 Q114 — If T, S and X are the only members of B, who is on C?**

|B| = 3 → |A| = 4 → |C| = 2. R must be on A. A cannot take Z or Y, so A = {W, V, U, R}.

> **Answer: C = {Z, Y} → option (1)**

&nbsp;

**(c) 2018 Q115 — If R is the only member of B, who is on A?**

|A| = 2, |C| = 6. T and S cannot be on C and are not on B.

> **Answer: T and S → option (4)**

&nbsp;

**(d) 2020 Q22 — What is the largest number that can serve together on C?**

T, S, R can never be on C, so at most six. It works: A = {T, S}, B = {R}, C = everyone else.

> **Answer: 6 → option (3)**

## Question 5 · Logical puzzle set — "A to G travelling in three vehicles"

**Appeared in:** NIMCET 2024 (Q59, Q61, Q63) → NIMCET 2025 (Q104) · gap 1 year

&nbsp;

A, B, C, D, E, F and G are travelling in three vehicles — a Swift, a Creta and a Nexon — with at least two passengers in each. Only two of them are male. Among them there are two engineers, two doctors and three teachers.

1. C is a lady doctor and does not travel with the sisters A and F.
2. B, a male engineer, travels with only G, a teacher, in the Swift.
3. D is a male doctor.
4. Two persons of the same profession do not travel in the same vehicle.
5. A is not an engineer and travels in the Creta.
6. The sisters A and F travel in the same vehicle.

&nbsp;

### Solution

**Step 1.** The Swift holds exactly B and G. A and F are in the Creta.

**Step 2.** C is not with A and F, so C is in the Nexon. D is a doctor and cannot share with C (also a doctor); the Swift is full → **D is in the Creta**.

**Step 3.** The Nexon needs a second passenger, and only E is left → **E is in the Nexon**.

**Step 4.** Professions used so far: B engineer, G teacher, C doctor, D doctor. Left for A, E, F: one engineer and two teachers. A is not an engineer → A is a teacher. F is in the Creta with A, so F cannot also be a teacher → **F is the engineer**, E is a teacher.

| Vehicle | Passengers | Professions |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Swift | B, G | engineer, teacher |
| Creta | A, F, D | teacher, engineer, doctor |
| Nexon | C, E | doctor, teacher |

&nbsp;

**(a) What is F's profession?**

> **Answer: Engineer → 2024 Q59 option (4), 2025 Q104 option (3)**

&nbsp;

**(b) In which vehicle does C travel?**

> **Answer: Nexon → 2024 Q61 option (3)**

&nbsp;

**(c) Who are the three teachers?**

> **Answer: G, E, A → 2024 Q63 option (2) "GEA"**

(The 2024 paper said "only one of them is a male" — a misprint, since B and D are both male. The 2025 paper corrected it to two.)

## Question 6 · Probability — Bayes' theorem

**Appeared in:** NIMCET 2019 (Q13) → NIMCET 2023 (Q103) · gap 4 years

&nbsp;

A computer factory has two plants. Plant T1 produces 20% and plant T2 produces 80% of the computers. 7% of all computers produced are defective. It is known that

P(defective | made in T1) = 10 × P(defective | made in T2).

A computer is chosen at random and turns out **not** to be defective. The probability that it was made in plant T2 is

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) 36⁄73

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) 47⁄79

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) 78⁄93

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(4) 75⁄83

&nbsp;

### Solution

**Step 1.** Let P(D | T2) = p. Then P(D | T1) = 10p.

**Step 2.** Total probability of a defective computer:

0.07 = 0.2 × 10p + 0.8 × p = 2.8p  ⟹  p = 0.025, so P(D | T1) = 0.25.

**Step 3.** P(not defective) = 1 − 0.07 = 0.93, and P(not defective | T2) = 1 − 0.025 = 0.975.

**Step 4.** Bayes:

P(T2 | not defective) = (0.8 × 0.975) ⁄ 0.93 = 0.78 ⁄ 0.93 = **78⁄93**

> **Answer: (3) — 78⁄93** (same option in both years)

## Question 7 · Calculus — tangent to a parametric curve

**Appeared in:** NIMCET 2015 (Q45) → NIMCET 2023 (Q112) · gap 8 years

&nbsp;

The equation of the tangent at any point of the curve x = a cos 2t, y = 2√2 a sin t, with m as its slope, is

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) y = mx + a(m − 1⁄m)

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) y = mx − a(m + 1⁄m)

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) y = mx + a(a + 1⁄a)

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(4) y = amx + a(m − 1⁄m)

&nbsp;

### Solution

**Step 1.** Differentiate: dx⁄dt = −2a sin 2t = −4a sin t cos t, and dy⁄dt = 2√2 a cos t.

**Step 2.** Slope:

m = dy⁄dx = (2√2 a cos t) ⁄ (−4a sin t cos t) = −1 ⁄ (√2 sin t)  ⟹  sin t = −1 ⁄ (√2 m)

**Step 3.** Point of contact in terms of m:

y₀ = 2√2 a sin t = −2a⁄m,  x₀ = a cos 2t = a(1 − 2 sin²t) = a(1 − 1⁄m²)

**Step 4.** Tangent y − y₀ = m(x − x₀):

y + 2a⁄m = mx − am + a⁄m  ⟹  **y = mx − a(m + 1⁄m)**

> **Answer: (2)** (same option in both years)

**Shortcut.** Eliminate t: sin t = y⁄(2√2 a) and cos 2t = 1 − 2 sin²t give x = a − y²⁄(4a), i.e. the parabola y² = −4a(x − a). Its slope-form tangent is y = m(x − a) − a⁄m — the same answer in one line.

## Question 8 · Coding–decoding

**Appeared in:** NIMCET 2015 (Q35) → NIMCET 2016 (Q37) · gap 1 year

&nbsp;

If the word EXAMINATION is coded as 56149512965, then the word GOVERNMENT is coded as

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;2015: (1) 7645954552 (2) 7654694562 (3) 7645955423 (4) 7654964526

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;2016: (1) 7655955552 (2) 7645954552 (3) 7645954452 (4) 7644956552

&nbsp;

### Solution

**Step 1.** Line up letters and digits: E→5, X→6, A→1, M→4, I→9, N→5, A→1, T→2, I→9, O→6, N→5.

**Step 2.** Find the rule. E is the 5th letter → 5. X is the 24th → 2 + 4 = 6. N is the 14th → 1 + 4 = 5. T is the 20th → 2. So each digit is the **digit-sum of the letter's position in the alphabet**.

**Step 3.** Apply to GOVERNMENT: G(7)→7, O(15)→6, V(22)→4, E(5)→5, R(18)→9, N(14)→5, M(13)→4, E→5, N→5, T(20)→2.

> **Answer: 7645954552 → 2015 option (1), 2016 option (2)**

## Question 10 · Arithmetic puzzle

**Appeared in:** NIMCET 2017 (Q105) → NIMCET 2018 (Q89) · gap 1 year

&nbsp;

A caterpillar crawls up a pole 75 inches high, starting from the ground. Each day it crawls up 5 inches and each night it slides down 4 inches. When will it reach the top of the pole?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) at the end of 70 days

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) at the end of 71 days

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) at the end of 72 days

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(4) at the end of 73 days

&nbsp;

### Solution

**Step 1.** Each full day-and-night the caterpillar gains 5 − 4 = 1 inch. But it reaches the top *during a day's climb*, before the night's slide — so do not divide 75 by 1.

**Step 2.** At the start of day n it stands at (n − 1) inches. After that day's climb it is at n − 1 + 5 = n + 4 inches.

**Step 3.** Reach the top when n + 4 ≥ 75, i.e. n = 71.

> **Answer: (2) — at the end of 71 days** (same option in both years)

**Trap:** 75 − 5 = 70 forgets that the final climb still needs its own day.

## Question 11 · Coordinate geometry — locus

**Appeared in:** NIMCET 2015 (Q70) → NIMCET 2023 (Q57) · gap 8 years

&nbsp;

A circle touches the x-axis and also touches the circle with centre (0, 3) and radius 2. The locus of the centre of the first circle is

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;a parabola &nbsp;·&nbsp; a hyperbola &nbsp;·&nbsp; a circle &nbsp;·&nbsp; an ellipse

&nbsp;

### Solution

**Step 1.** Let the centre be (h, k) with k > 0. Touching the x-axis means the radius equals k.

**Step 2.** Touching the given circle externally means the distance between centres equals the sum of radii, k + 2:

h² + (k − 3)² = (k + 2)²

**Step 3.** Expand: h² + k² − 6k + 9 = k² + 4k + 4  ⟹  **h² = 10k − 5**, which is a parabola.

(If the contact is internal, h² + (k − 3)² = (k − 2)² gives h² = 2k − 5 — also a parabola.)

> **Answer: a parabola → 2015 option (1), 2023 option (3)**

## Question 12 · Syllogism

**Appeared in:** NIMCET 2018 (Q96) → NIMCET 2022 (Q71) · gap 4 years (also NIMCET 2013 Q71)

&nbsp;

Statements:
- All mangoes are golden in colour.
- No golden-coloured things are cheap.

Conclusions:
- I. All mangoes are cheap.
- II. Golden-coloured mangoes are not cheap.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Only I follows &nbsp;·&nbsp; Only II follows &nbsp;·&nbsp; Either I or II follows &nbsp;·&nbsp; Neither follows

&nbsp;

### Solution

**Step 1.** Mangoes ⊂ Golden, and Golden ∩ Cheap = ∅. So Mangoes ∩ Cheap = ∅: **no mango is cheap**.

**Step 2.** Conclusion I ("all mangoes are cheap") contradicts this — rejected. Conclusion II says the golden mangoes (which is every mango) are not cheap — follows directly.

> **Answer: Only conclusion II follows → 2018 option (2), 2022 option (3)**

## Question 13 · Arithmetic — percentages with a ratio

**Appeared in:** NIMCET 2018 (Q76) → NIMCET 2020 (Q10) · gap 2 years

&nbsp;

In a city 40% of the adults are illiterate while 85% of the children are literate. If the ratio of adults to children is 2 : 3, what percent of the population is literate?

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(1) 20%

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(2) 25%

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(3) 50%

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;(4) 75%

&nbsp;

### Solution

**Step 1.** Take 2 adults and 3 children — 5 people in all.

**Step 2.** Literate adults = 60% of 2 = 1.2. Literate children = 85% of 3 = 2.55.

**Step 3.** Total literate = 3.75 out of 5 = **75%**.

> **Answer: (4) — 75%** (same option in both years)

(The 2020 paper changed the figures to 40.1% and 85.1%: 0.599 × 2 + 0.851 × 3 = 3.751 of 5 ≈ 75% — same option.)

## Question 14 · Computer awareness

**Appeared in:** NIMCET 2008 (Q110) → NIMCET 2017 (Q30) · gap 9 years

&nbsp;

The first instruction of the bootstrap loader program of an operating system is stored in

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;RAM &nbsp;·&nbsp; BIOS &nbsp;·&nbsp; Hard Disk &nbsp;·&nbsp; None of these

&nbsp;

### Solution

At power-on RAM is empty and the hard disk cannot be read until a driver is running. The CPU therefore fetches its very first instructions from **ROM firmware — the BIOS** (the reset vector). The BIOS then loads the boot sector from the disk into RAM and hands over.

> **Answer: BIOS → 2008 option (2), 2017 option (3)**
